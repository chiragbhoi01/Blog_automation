Demoly Vertical 5: Final Industry-Specific Master Writing Blueprint
1. Purpose & Editorial Intent
Vertical 5 articles target operators, technical leads, and founders searching for tools that fit the real constraints, regulations, and workflows of their specific sector.
The article must connect five steps directly:
Industry Context⟶Operational Problem⟶Specific Demands⟶Candidate Tools⟶Practical Decision
The reader must feel that the article was written by someone who understands their daily work, not generated by swapping an industry name into a generic listicle.
2. Core Editorial Rule: Minimize
Target Length: Approximately 1,200–1,500 words by default.
Shorter is preferred: If an industry workflow and its top tools can be thoroughly evaluated in 1,100–1,300 words, end the article there.
Information Density > Word Count: Never pad an article to hit an arbitrary word count.
Zero Repetition: If an operational detail is stated in a tool profile, do not explain it again in the comparison table, decision section, or FAQs. Every section must perform a distinct job:
Tool Profile: Explains what the tool is, why it fits the industry, its core strengths, practical limits, and pricing.
Comparison Table: Provides a quick side-by-side reference across sector-specific criteria.
Decision Section: Maps specific tools to distinct operational scenarios.
FAQs: Resolves common edge-case objections in 1–2 direct sentences.
3. Language & Tone Standards
Simpler Words: Use plain, direct words over complicated phrasing (use instead of utilize, help instead of facilitate, show instead of demonstrate, start instead of commence).
Plain Descriptions: State practical outcomes directly.
Write: "Show the client how the website works."
Avoid: "Facilitate comprehensive post-delivery knowledge transfer."
Write: "Help clients find answers without another call."
Avoid: "Reduce dependency on synchronous post-launch communication."
Sentence & Paragraph Length: Write complete, natural sentences. Paragraphs should be concise (2–4 sentences, under 65 words).
Em-Dash Policy: Minimize em-dashes (—). Replace them with commas, colons, parentheses, or separate sentences.
Technical Terms: Use genuine industry terms (e.g., DNS, webhooks, CMS collections, PII, PHI, FHIR, MRN, SCIM) where appropriate, but explain their practical impact simply.
Banish Marketing Fluff: Cut buzzwords like game-changer, revolutionary, seamless, empower, and next-level.
4. Master Article Architecture
Every article in Vertical 5 follows an 8-part progression:
H1: Best [Tool / Handover / Walkthrough / Demo / Doc Tool] for [Target Industry]
├── Introduction (80–100 words: Failure Point + Why Tool Choice Matters + Quick TL;DR)
├── What [Industry] Needs From a [Tool Type] (150–200 words: 3–4 Sector Pillars)
├── Best [Tool Type] for [Industry] (Short tool profiles, ~100–150 words each)
│   ├── 1. Demoly (or Primary Category Specialist)
│   ├── 2. [Tool 2 Name]
│   ├── 3. [Tool 3 Name]
│   └── 4–5. [Additional category-distinct candidates]
├── [Industry] Tool Comparison Table (Compact Markdown matrix)
├── Privacy, Security & Compliance Requirements (Mandatory for regulated sectors)
├── Real-World Industry Scenarios (2–3 grounded, realistic situations)
├── Choosing the Right Tool for Your [Industry / Team] (Short intro + 2–4 bullets + closing)
└── Frequently Asked Questions (Exactly 5 questions, 1–2 sentences each, final section)

5. Section-by-Section Specifications
H1: Target Title
Use the exact target keyword or an optimized, human-readable variation (e.g., Best Client Handover Tool for Web Development Agencies).
Introduction
Length: 80–100 words across 2 short paragraphs.
Core Tasks:
Open directly at the specific operational failure point in that sector.
Explain why tool choice matters and what generic software misses.
Include a bulleted TL;DR: Quick Comparison assigning each profiled tool to its core operational niche.
Include Visual Reference #1 contrasting the traditional friction point against modern self-serve delivery.
Avoid: Long category histories, broad definitions, or mentioning Demoly in the first sentence.
What [Industry] Needs From a [Tool Type]
Length: 150–200 words.
Format: A 1-sentence lead-in followed by 3–4 concise bullet points with bold lead-in anchors (* **Anchor Name:** Practical explanation).
Content: Include only criteria that materially affect the buying decision in this sector (e.g., permanent canvas redaction for legal tech; copyable terminal code for bootcamps; zero-account client access for web agencies).
Best [Tool Type] for [Industry] (Tool Teardowns)
Candidate Count: 4 to 6 category-distinct platforms. Do not include redundant clones or weak tools just to pad the list.
Length per Profile: ~100–150 words each.
Sub-element Hierarchy:
What it is: 1 concise sentence explaining the product and its capture format.
Why it fits [Industry]: 2–3 sentences connecting its capabilities directly to the sector's daily workflow.
Key strengths: 2–3 concise bullets with bold lead-ins.
Limitations: 1 honest bullet stating practical boundaries.
Pricing: 1 concise summary of free tier, starting paid tier, and viewer-seat terms. Append: *(Note: Competitor pricing is subject to vendor updates.)*
Best for: 1 clear sentence specifying the exact team, studio, or workflow that benefits most.
Visual Reference: 1 descriptive callout showing the tool executing a sector-specific task.
Comparison Table
Structure: A compact Markdown table.
Columns: 5 to 6 meaningful, industry-specific criteria.
Recommended Columns: Tool | Primary Role | Search & Interactivity | Client / Viewer Access | Redaction / Privacy | Pricing Model
Cell Content Standard: Short 3–8 word outcome phrases. Never use naked checkmarks ("✓"), crosses ("✗"), or simple "Yes/No" labels.
Privacy, Security & Compliance Requirements (Adaptive Section)
When to Include: Mandatory for regulated or sensitive sectors (Legal Tech, Fintech, Healthcare, Enterprise IT, HR Tech). Omit or integrate into criteria for standard creative and marketing agencies.
Content Standard: Explain the real operational risk (e.g., exposing client PII, patient MRNs, or production API tokens).
Technical Distinction: Clearly distinguish between client-side CSS blurs (inspectable in dev tools) and permanent, frame-level canvas redaction.
Legal Guardrail: Never claim a tool is "100% compliant" in the abstract. State specific technical capabilities (e.g., "offers HIPAA BAA coverage on enterprise plans," "removes sensitive data at the canvas layer before rendering").
Real-World Industry Scenarios
Length: 120–180 words.
Content: 2–3 concise scenarios demonstrating how different tools resolve specific workplace edge cases.
Grounding: Reference realistic interfaces and operations (e.g., updating a Webflow CMS collection, testing a Stripe webhook in staging, or redacting an account number in an e-discovery platform).
Choosing the Right Tool for Your [Industry / Team]
Length: 80–120 words.
Structure:
1–2 line introductory lead-in.
3–4 concise bullets formatted as:
* Choose [Tool A] if you need...
* Choose [Tool B] if your primary goal is...
* Choose Demoly if you need...
1–2 short closing lines explaining how modern teams stack complementary tools.
Rule: Do not rank tools or declare an arbitrary universal winner.
Frequently Asked Questions
Placement: The absolute final substantive section of the article.
Quantity: Exactly 5 questions addressing genuine search intent and edge-case objections.
Answer Length: 1–2 short sentences (maximum 3 lines on desktop, under 45 words per answer).
Rule: Direct and definitive; never repeat paragraphs from the main body.
6. Demoly Product Integration Protocol
Demoly must appear as a specialized, objective option built for specific workflows, not as an aggressive sales pitch:
Natural Placement: Profile Demoly based on its actual strengths (searchable browser walkthroughs, permanent canvas redaction, zero-account client access).
Honest Boundaries: Clearly state where Demoly is not the right tool (e.g., it is built for browser workflows, not native desktop OS recording, terminal screencasts, or multi-track studio timeline editing).
Accurate Pricing: Free plan (0forever,15-minutecap,5AI-assistedrecordings/month,unlimitedfreeviewers);Proplan(8/creator/month, 30-minute cap, unlimited AI search, unlimited free viewers).
Collective Language: When discussing broader category trends, reference platforms collectively: "Modern walkthrough tools like Demoly and other specialized async platforms allow teams to..."
7. Visual Reference System (Mandatory Format)
To maintain clean publishing across standard content management systems without rendering errors, use standardized bracketed visual callouts:
Plaintext
[Visual Reference: Brief, specific description of what the visual should show]

Visual 1 (Introduction): An infographic flowchart contrasting traditional industry delivery friction against modern searchable workflows.
Visual 2 to N (Inside Tool Profiles): A specific UI description showing each profiled platform executing a sector-specific task.
Visual (Comparison Table): A high-contrast matrix summary.
Rule: Never include generic stock photos; describe actual interface states and user interactions.
8. Plain-Editor Formatting Standards
All articles must be fully compatible with basic web content editors (WordPress, Webflow, Ghost, or Google Docs):
Allowed Formatting: Standard headings (# H1, ## H2, ### H3), short paragraphs, bulleted lists, numbered lists, simple Markdown tables, bold lead-in anchors, and bracketed visual references.
Strictly Prohibited:
No triple-backtick Markdown code blocks (```).
No Markdown-formatted ticket schemas or fake terminal logs.
No complex, nested, or platform-dependent callout syntax.
If a detailed example is needed, describe it using a [Visual Reference: ...] callout instead.

10. Pre-Publication Quality Checklist
Before finalizing any article in Vertical 5, verify that it passes every check:
[ ] Word Count: Within or close to the 1,200–1,500 word range.
[ ] Industry Focus: Focuses on real sector challenges and workflows, avoiding generic advice with an industry name inserted.
[ ] No Repetition: Information in tool sections is not repeated verbatim in the comparison table, decision bullets, or FAQs.
[ ] Language Standard: Written in simple, direct English (use, help, show) with complete sentences and short paragraphs.
[ ] Em-Dash Minimization: Em-dashes (—) are minimized and replaced with commas, colons, parentheses, or restructured phrasing.
[ ] Concise Profiles: Tool profiles are kept to ~100–150 words covering what it is, industry fit, key strengths, limitations, pricing, and best fit.
[ ] Table Standard: Markdown table cells contain 3–8 word outcome phrases rather than bare checkmarks or "Yes/No" labels.
[ ] Demoly Balance: Demoly is positioned based on its actual browser-workflow strengths, includes honest boundaries, and uses collective category phrasing where appropriate.
[ ] Visual References: Includes clear, descriptive visual callouts using the standard [Visual Reference: ...] format.
[ ] Plain-Editor Compatibility: Free of triple-backtick code blocks and complex nested markdown.
[ ] Final FAQ Section: Exactly 5 questions appear as the final substantive section, with answers kept to 1–2 short sentences (≤45 words).
