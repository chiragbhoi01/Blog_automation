Vertical 1 Master Blueprint: How-To / Feature Content
1. Purpose of This Vertical
Vertical 1 delivers direct, tactical execution guides. Every article in this vertical answers a single practitioner question:
"How do I do this specific task right now?"
The reader arrives with an active browser tab, an unresolved problem, and zero patience for background theory. The article must serve as a scannable, frictionless operational walkthrough that takes them from starting point to completed, verified task with the minimum necessary words.
Strict Boundary Rules
Vertical 1 content explicitly does not:
Evaluate or rank competing market alternatives (belongs to Vertical 2 / 2C).
Prescribe organizational strategies, commercial frameworks, or holistic playbooks (belongs to Vertical 6).
Examine the software market to determine the best tool for an archetype (belongs to Vertical 4).
Investigate technical architecture, vector pipelines, or DOM event models under the hood (belongs to Vertical 7).
2. Core Editorial Philosophy
Every article in Vertical 1 adheres to five non-negotiable operational principles:
Action First: Reach the first procedural action within the first 100 words. The reader should never scroll past multiple paragraphs of introductory fluff to discover where to click.
Practical Over Theoretical: Explain only enough context for the reader to understand what they are about to execute. Omit category history, SaaS commentary, and industry generalities.
Short but Complete: Include every critical setting, UI element, and verification checkpoint required to complete the task. Eliminate anything that does not directly prevent failure.
Simple, Everyday Language: Write like an experienced colleague demonstrating a workflow on their monitor. Technical credibility comes from mechanical precision, not dense academic vocabulary.
Visual Where Possible: If an annotated screenshot, UI callout, or GIF explains an interface target faster than text, deploy the visual and keep the text concise.
One Article = One Task: Never broaden a narrow how-to query into a sprawling guide. Solve the stated task, confirm the result, and conclude.
3. Article Length & Complexity Bands
Vertical 1 rejects arbitrary word count minimums. Quality is measured by task resolution speed and clarity, not text volume. Writers must map topics to one of three operational bands:
Band A: Simple UI Tasks (600 – 900 words)
Characteristics: Single-screen adjustments, setting toggles, direct browser actions, basic link controls.
Topic Examples: Topic #5 (Cut Long Pauses), Topic #10 (Viewer-Only Access), Topic #11 (Revoke Access), Topic #18 (Edit Published Video), Topic #19 (Undo Edits).
Band B: Medium Multi-Step Workflows (900 – 1,200 words)
Characteristics: Core in-editor workflows, masking actions, timeline cuts, prompt querying, or multi-asset bundling.
Topic Examples: Topic #1 (Record Searchable Walkthrough), Topic #2 (Redact Sensitive Data), Topic #3 (Mask Passwords/API Keys), Topic #4 (Trim Mistakes), Topic #8 (Share Without Download), Topic #13 (Pin a Comment), Topic #21 (Summarize Walkthrough).
Band C: Multi-Environment & Repurposing Workflows (1,200 – 1,500 words)
Characteristics: Workflows spanning multiple browser contexts, developer diagnostics, multi-language localization, or automated documentation compilation.
Topic Examples: Topic #9 (Multi-Tab Recording), Topic #16 (Translate into 30+ Languages), Topic #17 (Bug Reproduction Context), Topic #20 (Turn Video into Documentation), Topic #24 (Search Across Video Library).
Rule of Minimization: If an article in Band B completely and safely solves the task in 850 words, finish it there. Never add filler, unnecessary background, or repetitive tips to reach an arbitrary target.

Section Breakdown & Rules
H1 Title: Matches the target search query directly (e.g., How to Redact Sensitive Client Data Before Sharing a Screen Recording). Avoid hype, sensationalized adjectives, or clickbait.
Introduction (60–90 words): Open directly on the operational friction point. State what the reader will accomplish and move immediately to the steps.
Quick Steps: A tight, numbered 4-to-5 item summary placed immediately below the intro. Formatted with bold lead-in action anchors for Google Featured Snippet capture.
Before You Start (Conditional): Include only when the user requires specific prerequisites (browser permissions, account roles, or pre-configured software tabs). Skip entirely if the task can begin immediately.
Step-by-Step Instructions: The functional core of the article (60–75% of total text). Chronologically ordered with active headings.
How to Verify It Worked: A 2-to-3 sentence test allowing the reader to confirm the workflow succeeded before sharing with external stakeholders (e.g., verifying links in an incognito window).
Important Notes & Edge Cases: 2 to 3 bullet points warning about practical limitations, performance habits, or recipient view conditions.
Troubleshooting (Conditional): Deployed only when the workflow has predictable technical failure points (e.g., tab audio dropouts, dynamic element shifts). Formatted strictly as Problem  Fix.
Conclusion (40–60 words): A concise operational closing confirming the reader's new capability. Naturally anchored by Demoly's free tier access ($0 forever, no credit card required).
Frequently Asked Questions: Exactly 4 to 5 granular questions positioned as the final substantive section.
5. Step-by-Step Framework & Writing Rules
The body steps form the foundation of the tutorial. Every step must follow a strict, scannable action loop:
$$\textbf{Step Heading} \longrightarrow \textbf{Direct Action Sentence} \longrightarrow \textbf{In-Step Visual Anchor} \longrightarrow \textbf{Expected State / Result}$$
The Three Step Components
Action-Oriented Heading: Numbered sequentially, leading with an active verb and naming the interface target.
Correct: Step 2: Highlight sensitive fields using the Redact tool
Incorrect: Step 2: Data privacy considerations in the interface
Step Body (2 to 4 sentences maximum):
Action: State the exact command (Click, Select, Drag, Toggle, Paste).
UI Element: Bold the exact name of the button, modal, or input field (Redact, Viewer Only, Start Recording).
Spatial Context: Clarify the physical position on the screen ("in the top-right toolbar", "in the left-hand navigation rail").
Result: State what happens when clicked ("A dark masking overlay appears across the selected container").
Visual Reference Anchor: Place a bracketed design instruction directly beneath the action text.
Step-Writing Execution Rules
Lead with Imperatives: Begin every instruction with a verb. Never write: "After deciding on your parameters, you should click the blue button." Write: "Click Save in the top-right corner to lock your edits."
Group Related Micro-Clicks: Do not create separate H2 steps for three clicks within the same modal. Group them logically using arrow paths: Settings > Access Permissions > Viewer Only.
Do Not Explain the Obvious: Omit basic computing instructions (e.g., how to click a mouse, how to press enter, how to open a browser window).
Single-Explanation Principle: If an interface mechanic is explained in Step 1, do not re-explain its utility in Step 3, the conclusion, or the FAQs.
Punctuation Discipline: Minimize em-dashes (—). Use commas, periods, colons, or parentheses to keep instructions clean and direct.
6. Standardized Visual Reference System
Visuals in Vertical 1 must carry functional weight. They must never serve as decorative stock filler.
Visual Reference Format
Every visual instruction must use this exact bracketed syntax:
Plaintext
[Visual Reference: Brief, specific description of what the visual shows, including active UI elements, highlighted bounding boxes, and the resulting state.]

Visual Standards
Tightly Cropped Over Full-Desktop: Crop to the relevant active panel, modal, or timeline handle. A full 4K screen capture makes buttons unreadable on mobile screens.
Callout Indicators: Specify highlighted colored borders, arrows, or callout boxes around the exact UI element being manipulated.
Before / After Splits: Use paired visual descriptions for tasks involving destructive edits, redactions, or automated silence removal.
Cadence: Maintain 1 visual reference per major procedural phase or every 2 to 3 micro-steps. Never stack multiple visual tags back-to-back without instructional text.

9. Tone, Vocabulary, and Readability Standards
Target Reading Level: Grade 7–8 readability paired with precise domain literacy.
Plain Vocabulary Replacements:
Use use instead of utilize.
Use help instead of facilitate.
Use show instead of demonstrate.
Use start instead of initiate or commence.
Use pick or choose instead of opt for.
Sentence & Paragraph Limits: Sentences must average 14 to 18 words. Paragraphs must never exceed 3 to 4 sentences (under 65 words).
Eliminate Corporate Buzzwords: Banish seamlessly, powerhouse, game-changer, revolutionary, effortlessly, robust, intuitive, paradigm shift.
Technical Terms: Terms like DOM elements, API keys, webhook URLs, staging environments, PII, timestamp citations, and canvas redaction are encouraged when accurate, but explain their practical effect simply.
10. Plain-Editor Formatting Standards
All articles must paste cleanly into standard content management systems (Webflow, WordPress, Ghost, Google Docs) without layout breakages:
Allowed Formatting:
Standard Markdown headings (# H1, ## H2, ### H3).
Standard numbered lists (1., 2., 3.) and bulleted lists (*).
Inline bolding for UI elements and lead-in anchors (**Button Name**).
Standard bracketed visual reference tags ([Visual Reference: ...]).
Prohibited Formatting:
No triple-backtick Markdown code blocks (```) for general text, step instructions, or checklists.
No complex, multi-row Markdown comparison tables with paragraphs inside cells.
No raw HTML containers, custom CSS classes, or platform-specific callout shortcodes.
11. SEO, Search Intent & Internal Linking
Search Intent Focus: Target informational, procedural "How-To" queries exclusively. The searcher wants to perform an action, not read an essay.
Featured Snippet Engineering: The Quick Steps block must appear within the first 120 words, formatted as an ordered list with bold lead-ins for snippet extraction.
Natural Keyword Integration: Integrate natural secondary variations (e.g., screen capture, web app walkthrough, mask API keys, video timeline trim, zero-download video) organically across step descriptions and H2s. Avoid repetitive keyword stuffing.
Contextual Internal Linking Strategy:
Upstream Planning: Link to a Vertical 6 Playbook or Checklist in the intro/prep section (e.g., linking a handover video guide to the Complete Client Handover Checklist).
Downstream Architecture: Link to a Vertical 7 Technical Deep-Dive when explaining underlying mechanics (e.g., linking DOM actions to DOM Capture vs. Pixel Capture).
Commercial Next Steps: Link to a Vertical 4 Best Tool guide or Vertical 2 Comparison in the conclusion or FAQs.
Limit: 3 to 5 internal links per article. Never place links inside direct step instructions where they pull the reader out of the workflow.
12. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ) Architecture
Strict Placement: Positioned at the very end of the article, immediately following the conclusion.
Quantity: Exactly 4 to 5 questions.
Answer Length: 1 to 3 sentences (under 45 words per answer).
Intent Purpose: FAQs must resolve specific operational edge cases, permission limits, viewer behaviors, and browser limitations (e.g., "Can viewers inspect redacted text in Chrome Developer Tools?" or "Does the recipient need a Demoly account to search?").
Anti-Repetition Rule: FAQs must never summarize or re-list the steps already explained in the main tutorial.
Revision Rule: When shortening an existing article, do not edit or cut the FAQ section unless the questions themselves contain inaccuracies or fluff. Focus all compression passes on the main body.
13. Editorial Anti-Patterns
Writers must avoid these recurring errors:
The Encyclopedic Intro: Writing two paragraphs about why client communication or video documentation is important before introducing Step 1.
Vague UI Directives: Writing "Go to settings and choose the appropriate option" instead of "Click Settings in the left rail and toggle Viewer Only to on."
The Decorative Screenshot: Adding visual references of generic stock images, empty browser tabs, or unannotated screens.
Step Fragmentation: Splitting a single 2-click setting adjustment into three separate H2 steps.
The Premature Sales Pitch: Mentioning Demoly's pricing, features, or value proposition in the opening sentence.
Prose Table Clones: Repeating identical information across the quick steps, body steps, and conclusion.
FAQ Restatements: Using the FAQ section to restate "How do I record my screen?" instead of answering granular edge-case objections.
