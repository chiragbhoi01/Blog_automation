Vertical 2B: Competitor Pricing Pages Editorial Blueprint & Writing Specification
1. Editorial Purpose of This Vertical
Moving Beyond the Official Pricing Page
A competitor’s official pricing page is designed for conversion: it highlights features, minimizes boundaries, buries seat penalties, and obscures the operational point where a tier breaks.
A Demoly Competitor Pricing article operates as an independent, forensic buying audit. It translates a vendor’s public numbers into actual cost of ownership, operational limits, workflow trade-offs, and team suitability.

Core Search Intent & Reader Takeaway
Readers searching [Competitor] pricing are in the late commercial investigation / buying stage. They want to answer five direct questions:
What will this actually cost my team each month and year?


What hard limits will I hit on the free or entry-level plan?


What features force an upgrade to the next tier?


Is this tool worth the money for my specific operational setup?


When does an alternative tool make more economic or functional sense?


Value-Add Over Generic Content
Forensic Pricing Math: Calculating the true cost across typical team sizes (e.g., a 10-person agency paying an AI seat markup).
Operational Boundary Analysis: Pinpointing the exact threshold where an asset fails (e.g., a 5-minute limit making client handovers impossible).
Balanced Workflow Fit: Outlining clear scenarios where the competitor is well worth the cost versus scenarios where its core format creates delivery friction.

4. Pricing Table System
The pricing table uses a flipped layout: plans run as columns across the top, while comparison criteria run as rows down the left.
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Criteria           │ Free / Tier 1    │ Tier 2 (Pro)     │ Tier 3 (Advanced)│ Enterprise     │
├────────────────────┼──────────────────┼──────────────────┼──────────────────┼────────────────┤
│ Monthly Billing    │ $0               │ $X / user / mo   │ $Y / user / mo   │ Custom quote   │
│ Annual Billing     │ $0               │ $X / user / mo   │ $Y / user / mo   │ Custom quote   │
│ Primary Limit      │ Limit metric     │ Unconstrained    │ Unconstrained    │ Unconstrained  │
│ Secondary Limit    │ Duration/Seats   │ Higher cap       │ Custom cap       │ Custom cap     │
│ Add-on / AI Status │ None             │ None             │ Included         │ Included       │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

Rules for Table Execution
Why This Orientation Works: Readers evaluate software by scanning across tiers horizontally to see where limits disappear and price steps occur.
Optimal Dimensions: 4 to 5 columns maximum (e.g., Free, Business, Business + AI, Enterprise). 5 to 6 criteria rows.
Criteria Selection: Include only criteria that define pricing boundaries:
Monthly Billing
Annual Billing
Primary Volume Metric (videos, guides, steps, or demo views)
Session/Duration Limit (minutes, flows, active sandboxes)
Key Technology Inclusion (AI suite, custom branding, or HTML export)
Cell Detail: Keep entries tight (2–5 words per cell, such as $18 / user / month or 25 videos per person).
Handling Complex Pricing Models:
Sales-Gated / Enterprise (e.g., Walnut, Navattic): Label column as Enterprise and state Custom quote or Starts ~$Xk/yr (annual contract) with an explanatory footnote.
Usage-Based / Credits: State base creator seat cost, then define credit allotments cleanly in the criteria row.
Mandatory Disclaimer: Always place below the table: *(Note: Pricing and plan terms are subject to vendor updates.)*


5. Plan Breakdown Rules
Each tier gets an H3 subsection under the ## [Tool] Pricing Plans container. Every tier follows a strict four-bullet structure:

* **Important limits:** [Specific ceilings: recording caps, user rules, missing features].

Content & Information Density Rules
Density Target: 70–100 words per tier. Total section: 350–500 words.
No Marketing Feature Dumps: Include only capabilities that define the tier's operational value. Exclude standard platform baselines (e.g., "secure login," "cloud storage").
Limits Must Be Explicit: Name exact figures (e.g., capped at 25 recordings per creator, 5-minute recording cap, 720p maximum quality).

The Four Core Operational Boundaries to Analyze
Volume & Duration Step-Ups: Explain what happens when a team outgrows entry-level caps (e.g., having to delete older videos to record new ones).
Feature & Technology Splits: Break down features locked behind premium tiers (e.g., advanced AI features requiring an extra $6/seat/month, increasing a 10-person team's bill by $600/year).
Seat Economics & Viewer Licensing: Explain who requires a paid license versus who can access assets for free (e.g., external clients can watch for free, but any internal collaborator who records requires a paid creator seat).
Export, Resolution, & Quality Controls: Detail technical output limits (e.g., 720p vs. 4K, watermarks, or local MP4 exports).

10. Writing Style and Readability Rules
The tone is that of an experienced technical operator auditing software costs for their own organization.
Use Simpler Words:
Use use instead of utilize.
Use help instead of facilitate.
Use show instead of demonstrate.
Use start instead of commence or initiate.
Use pick or choose instead of opt for.
Sentence Construction: Write complete, natural sentences (14–20 words average). Avoid choppy fragments and artificial rhetorical questions.
Paragraph Density: 2 to 3 lines per paragraph on desktop (maximum 4 lines).
No Em-Dashes: Minimize em-dashes (—). Use colons, parentheses, commas, or restructured sentences instead.
Accessible Technical Language: Technical does not mean complicated. Use terms like SAML SSO, SCIM provisioning, DOM events, API keys, Webhooks, canvas-layer redaction, and staging environments, but explain their cost and operational impact in plain English.
Banish Jargon: Eliminate empty SaaS marketing filler (seamless, revolutionize, game-changer, powerful solution, next-level, fast-paced digital world).

13. Pricing Accuracy & Freshness Protocol
Verify Official Sources: Pull pricing directly from the vendor's official pricing page and documentation.
State Dual Billing: Always state both monthly billing and annualized discounted billing (e.g., $18/user/month billed monthly, or $15/user/month billed annually).
Currency Standard: Default to USD ($) unless analyzing a regional-specific tool.
Flag Enterprise Packaging: For tools with gated enterprise tiers (e.g., Walnut, Navattic), state Custom quote and reference verified procurement ranges (e.g., Vendr market data) only when reliably verified.
Mandatory Disclaimer: Every pricing table and breakdown section must note that vendor packaging changes over time.

17. Anti-Patterns: What NOT to Do
Do NOT Re-List the Pricing Table in Paragraphs: Supporting sections must provide operational interpretation, not repetitive lists of prices and features.
Do NOT Use Unflipped Tables: Do not put plans in rows and criteria in columns. Keep plans as columns across the top for easy scanning.
Do NOT Write Sales Pitches for Demoly: Demoly belongs only in the alternatives section as a specialized option for client walkthroughs.
Do NOT Make Universal Value Claims: Never claim a tool is "not worth it" or "the best on the market." Use scenario-based evaluation.
Do NOT Write Long FAQ Answers: Any FAQ answer that exceeds 3 lines on desktop must be edited down.
Do NOT Place FAQs Before the End: The FAQ section must remain the final substantive section of the article.
Do NOT Pad Word Counts: Do not add filler paragraphs to hit 2,000 words if a tool's pricing can be explained thoroughly in 1,300 words.
